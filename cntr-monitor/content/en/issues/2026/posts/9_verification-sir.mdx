title: Systems for Implementation Review
intro: "Systems for implementation review (SIRs) are international or regional institutions and mechanisms through which parties to a multilateral agreement share information on implementation, compare their implementation activities, review one another’s performance, learn from implementation experience, address noncompliance or poor compliance, and, where useful, adjust their commitments. They complement specific verification measures where these exist, and can help address problems of poor compliance where they do not. Experience, particularly from environmental regimes, shows that SIRs can be used to make a significant difference to the implementation and effectiveness of complex multilateral agreements. The main question addressed in this chapter is whether and how the further development and use of SIRs can improve the dynamism and effectiveness of existing and emerging multilateral arms control and disarmament regimes. This chapter argues that greater attention to developing and using SIRs in multilateral arms control regimes could make a difference, briefly illustrating this argument with reference to the Biological Weapons Convention (BWC), Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), the Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), and humanitarian arms control agreements, as well as initiatives to promote international cooperation on the peaceful uses of outer space and to address the use of cyberweapons."

<Leadin>The development and effectiveness </Leadin>of multilateral agreements depends not only on the international configurations of power and interests but also on how the agreement and its institutions have been designed and developed. Verification systems and SIRs are both examples of the importance of institutional design. Compared with verification, SIRs have received relatively little attention in the fields of arms control and disarmament. At a time when progress on arms control is particularly difficult, it is important to explore how the development or increased use of SIRs could be helpful.
