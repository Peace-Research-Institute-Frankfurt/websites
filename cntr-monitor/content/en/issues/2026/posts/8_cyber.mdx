title: What Cyberspace Regulation Can Learn from Traditional Arms Control
intro: 'Mechanisms from chemical weapons disarmament could inform future regulation of cyberspace. Despite growing cyber threats and the increasing military use of cyber operations, international regulation remains fragmented and largely nonbinding. Drawing on interdisciplinary expert interviews, this chapter identifies key challenges for cyberspace regulation, including dual-use technologies, verification difficulties, rapid technological change, and limited political will. The CWC—particularly its General-Purpose Criterion, attribution-related practices, independent expertise, and related informal practices—could provide valuable lessons and support a shift from technology-centered regulation toward behavior- and context-based governance in cyberspace.'

<Leadin>Can lessons </Leadin>from traditional Arms Control (AC), such as in relation to chemical weapons, offer useful guidance for future regulation of cyberspace despite the apparent distinctiveness of the weapon system involved? Drawing on expert interviews from the fields of cybersecurity, AC, and policy, this chapter argues that important conceptual lessons can be learned from traditional AC, as illustrated here using concepts from the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC).
